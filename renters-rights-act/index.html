The Renters' Rights Act, without the panic.

The biggest change to private renting in England in a generation took effect on 1 May 2026. Here is what it did, what it did not do, and what we do about it for you.

What changed on 1 May 2026.

Everything below applies to existing tenancies as well as new ones, from the same day. Notices served before then could be taken to court until 31 July 2026, and that window has closed.

Section 21 is gone

Possession now needs a ground under Section 8. The grounds were widened to include selling the property and moving in yourself or a close family member, but neither can take effect in the first twelve months of a tenancy, and notice periods are longer than they were. The notice is Form 3A.

Every tenancy is periodic

Fixed terms ended. Existing fixed-term tenancies converted automatically. Tenants can leave on two months' notice from day one, which puts more weight on choosing the right tenant and keeping them.

Rent rises once a year, by notice

A Section 13 notice on Form 4A, two months' notice, no more than once a year, and the tenant can challenge it at the First-tier Tribunal for a fee of £47. Rent review clauses in agreements no longer work.

No bidding wars, one month in advance

You must advertise a rent and cannot accept more. Rent in advance is capped at one month. Civil penalties for breaches run to £7,000, and to £40,000 for repeated or serious ones.

Pets, families and benefits

Tenants may request a pet and you need a reasonable ground to refuse within 28 days. Refusing applicants because they have children or receive benefits is unlawful.

The paperwork changed too

The How to Rent guide was withdrawn. New tenancies need a written statement of terms before they start, and existing tenants had to be sent the government's Information Sheet by 31 May 2026. A court will not now make a possession order on most grounds while a deposit is unprotected.

Still to come

The landlord register, called Register your rental property, opens on 15 December 2026 in the West Midlands and reaches every region by August 2027. Every landlord must be on it by 14 November 2027, at £65 per property per year. A mandatory ombudsman for landlords is expected from 2028. EPC C is confirmed for 1 October 2030 and the Decent Homes Standard for 2035. A consultation on extending Awaab's Law to private rentals closes on 18 December 2026.

Checked against gov.uk on 14 November 2026. General information, not legal advice. Our guides go into the detail.

What we do about it.

Compliance is not a bolt-on. It is the reason most landlords instruct a managing agent now.

At the start

  • Advertised rent set and held, no bids accepted
  • Applicant checks that are lawful and documented
  • Tenancy agreement drafted for periodic tenancies
  • Statutory documents served and receipts kept

Every year

  • Rent reviewed against the market and a Section 13 notice served correctly
  • Certificates renewed before they expire
  • Landlord register entries made when your region opens, and kept current
  • Pet requests answered inside 28 days with a reasoned decision

If it goes wrong

  • Arrears acted on from the first missed payment
  • The right Section 8 ground chosen and the notice served properly
  • Evidence file kept from day one so a claim is not lost on paperwork
  • Litigation support through our sister practice where a claim is needed
